Privacy Policy

Desktop Organizer is a local file tool: it reads the folders you tell it to read, writes its own records into your Windows user profile, and sends nothing to us. This policy states what is stored, what leaves your machine when you use AI features, and what this website does with a visitor's connection.

Your files stay on your PC

Desktop Organizer performs every file operation on the computer it is installed on. An Electron shell presents the interface and a local Python engine does the work; the engine's HTTP API binds to 127.0.0.1 only, so it can be reached by the interface on the same machine and by nothing else. There is no server operated by us, no account to log in to, and no queue your files pass through on the way to somewhere else.

What the engine does with your files

It works on the folders you enable — Desktop, Downloads, Documents, Pictures, Videos and Music, plus up to twelve custom folders — and sorts files into Documents, Images, Videos, Music, Archives, Code, Installers and Others, optionally routing them into a central OrganizerLibrary folder. Every one of those movements happens on your disk:

  • Sorting reads file names and extensions to decide a destination. The destination is a folder on your machine.
  • Duplicate detection compares file contents in chunks so large files can be matched without loading them whole. The comparison runs locally and the result is shown to you on screen.
  • Cleanup moves files you confirm into the Recycle Bin. The app does not permanently delete your files, and a recycled file stays on your disk until you empty the bin or Windows reclaims the space.
  • Organization History records which file moved from where to which folder in each run, with undo, so the trail is a local entry you can review and reverse.

Files that are never touched

Downloads in progress are protected. The engine ignores .crdownload, .partial, .part, .download, .opdownload and .tmp, and waits out a configurable file stabilization delay (2 seconds by default) and download completion delay (10 seconds by default) before filing anything. Nothing is uploaded while it waits, and no file is read into memory for transport anywhere.

What the app stores locally

The app writes its working data into the app data folder inside your Windows user profile, alongside your documents rather than on a remote system. That folder holds your settings (enabled folders, category routing, the custom folder list, monitoring delays, theme choices and AI provider profiles), a SQLite database holding the history entries and chat records the interface reads, the Organization History record that makes undo possible, the chat history of the AI workspace with its titles, pins and archives, and plain-text diagnostic logs written by the local engine.

None of it is transmitted to us

We do not receive your settings, your database, your history, your chats or your logs. If you choose to attach a log or a screenshot when reporting a problem, that is your decision and your action, taken through GitHub; the app never sends an attachment anywhere on its own.

Your data, your copy

Because the records live in your user profile, you control them directly: delete individual chats from the sidebar, clear history entries, or remove the app data folder altogether. Uninstalling through Settings > Apps > Installed apps removes the program, and the data folder is yours to keep or delete; v1.0.2 fixed a defect in how uninstall handled retained app data, so removal now behaves as documented here. There is no remote copy for you to ask us to erase.

Shared and managed computers

App data sits in the profile of the Windows account that installed the app. Anyone with access to that account, including software running under it, can read the stored settings, history, chats, logs and keys. On a domain-managed work machine, assume your administrators can reach it too. If that matters to you, use a local model profile and delete the app data folder when you finish.

No hidden cloud uploads

Stated plainly: the app does not upload anything to us. No file contents, no file names, no folder paths, no inventory of what you own, no screenshots, no clipboard contents, no machine identifiers. There is no background sync service, no "help us improve" channel, no silent crash bundle and no activation ping. The only outbound traffic the program produces is traffic you start yourself: the AI provider in your active profile, which receives the prompt text you send, and your own browser fetching an installer from GitHub.

Everything else — monitoring, sorting, duplicate detection, undo, history, recycle, the Ctrl + K command palette and the tray menu — runs with no network involvement. Install the app, disconnect from the internet, and the file engine behaves identically.

We do not sell user data

We do not sell, rent, trade, licence, broker or otherwise disclose your files, your file names, your folder structure, your history, your chats, your API keys or any other information produced by the app. There is no data-advertising business behind this project, no server holding your data and no partner list to publish. The code is MIT licensed and auditable in the open: the source repository.

API keys and provider profiles

To use the AI workspace with a hosted provider you supply your own key. That key, with the profile it belongs to — provider name, base URL, model choice — is written to the app data folder on your machine and nowhere else. It is not sent to us, not included in any diagnostic bundle, and not uploaded for backup or sync. The only place a key is ever transmitted is the provider endpoint you configured, in the header that provider requires to authenticate you.

No bundled credentials

The installer ships with no keys, no demo tokens, no shared account and no trial provider. A fresh install cannot make an AI call until you add a profile yourself, so nobody else's usage is attributed to your machine and your usage is never billed to someone else. Profiles for OpenRouter, OpenAI, Google AI Studio, Anthropic, LM Studio, Ollama or a custom OpenAI-compatible endpoint live on your machine only: switching between them sends neither your chats nor your other keys anywhere, and switching never loses a conversation.

Handling your key well

  • Give a key the narrowest permission set your provider allows, and revoke it when you stop using the app.
  • Do not share your app data folder, and do not paste its contents into a bug report.
  • Prefer a per-application key so that usage and spend stay attributable.
  • On a machine you do not fully control, use LM Studio or Ollama instead of a hosted provider.

Backups and disk imaging

Because keys are stored in your app data folder, anything that copies your user profile — a backup tool, a disk image, file-history software, a roaming profile — copies them too. That is a consequence of keeping your data local rather than on our servers, and it is worth knowing before you archive a machine.

AI prompts and third-party providers

The AI workspace is a chat interface plus a set of actions over your files. When you send a message, the prompt text goes to the provider in your active profile. File contents are included only if you attach them yourself; the app does not silently package up your folders to give the model context. Whatever a provider receives is governed by that provider's own privacy policy and your agreement with it, not by this one.

Local models keep everything on the machine

An LM Studio or Ollama profile routes requests to a model running on your own computer, and with those profiles no prompt text, no attachment and no file content leaves the machine — the closest thing this app has to a strictly private AI mode. A custom OpenAI-compatible endpoint sits in between: what leaves your machine goes to the base URL you typed in, which may be a company gateway, a self-hosted server or a reseller. Check that URL first.

Actions the assistant can take

Assistant actions are grouped into four safety classes, so you can see at a glance how much a suggestion is allowed to do:

  • Read only. Inspects information; changes nothing.
  • Launches something. Starts a program or opens a location.
  • Changes files. Renames, moves or organises on your disk.
  • Deletes files. Requires your explicit confirmation, and routes to the Recycle Bin rather than removing data outright.

Third-party providers are not us

OpenRouter, OpenAI, Google AI Studio, Anthropic, LM Studio, Ollama and any custom endpoint are independent services run by other parties, and their retention, logging and training use sit outside our control. Read each provider's terms and privacy notice before putting work documents, personal information, client material or credentials into a prompt or an attachment. We do not receive your prompts, your attachments, your completions or your usage records.

Telemetry

Desktop Organizer collects no telemetry. There is no analytics SDK, no crash reporting, no usage tracking, no feature counters, no session identifier, no device identifier and no installation identifier in the product. We cannot tell how many people run the app, how often a folder is organised, which provider someone picked or whether a run succeeded, and nothing in the codebase is built to find out.

  • No metrics or analytics library is shipped in the installer.
  • No automatic crash dump is uploaded; diagnostic logs stay in your app data folder.
  • No phone-home on install, first launch, update or window close.
  • No tracking of which folders you watch or which files you sort.

What that costs, and what it buys

The honest trade is that problems reach us only when you choose to describe them, so GitHub issues and your own reading of the log folder are how quality is maintained. In return, nothing about your habits accumulates on a server, and a network capture of the app at work shows no calls to us. If telemetry were ever added — there is no plan to add it — this page would have to change first, and the feature would have to be documented and visible rather than silent.

This website

This site is a static set of HTML files served by GitHub Pages. It sets no cookies, runs no analytics, embeds no tracking or advertising scripts, stores nothing in your browser, and offers no form to fill in and no account to create. Two ordinary parts of the modern web still see a visitor's connection, and it is fair to name them.

  • Web fonts. The page requests its fonts from the Google Fonts CDN, so that CDN's operator sees the IP address making the request along with the standard data of an ordinary asset fetch. The site needs a network connection for its fonts and falls back to a system font when it has none.
  • Installer downloads. The setup file is served from GitHub's release asset storage, and GitHub keeps ordinary server logs for those requests. We do not receive your IP address from a download and cannot see who downloaded the app.

No consent banner, because there is nothing to consent to

Beyond the two items above this page is a document, written for you to read rather than to measure you, and the behaviour of the product does not change depending on how you arrived here. A cookie notice on a site this simple would be theatre, so the facts are disclosed here instead of behind a popup.

Children's privacy

Desktop Organizer is a desktop file utility aimed at adults working on Windows 10 or 11. It is not directed at children, we do not market it to children, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from a child through the app or this website. Because the app needs no account and gathers no telemetry, it never asks for a name, an age, an email address or a birthday.

Two practical notes for a household that shares a computer. The AI workspace can be pointed at hosted providers whose own terms set minimum ages and content rules, and a minor using such a provider is dealing with that provider, so profiles should be configured by an adult or set to a local model. Everything the app records — history, chats, logs, keys — is readable by anyone signed in to that Windows account, which is worth thinking about before installing on a family machine. If you believe a child's information has reached us through a bug report or a message on the repository, contact us through GitHub issues and say so plainly; we will remove it.

Changes and contact

This policy lives with the repository, and the version date in the banner above is the reference point for what applied while you used a given build. Material changes — a new outbound destination, a new category of stored data, the introduction of anything resembling telemetry — are published here before the release that depends on them ships, with a note in the changelog. Continuing to use the app after a change is published means the updated policy is the one in force. Three commitments will not change quietly: file operations stay local and no file content is uploaded to us; API keys stay in your app data folder and reach only your chosen provider; and we do not sell user data.

Contact

Desktop Organizer is maintained by Ahmed-Coder under the MIT licence. The fastest route to a human is the repository: open an issue for a defect or a privacy question, or read the source before trusting it. Installer files, if you want to verify a build against this policy, are on the releases page. The documentation explains how the engine and the AI workspace are wired, and the terms of use set out the licence and the limits of responsibility.
